Round 4 · Mobile layouts for H

Four phone layouts for H. The open question is where Publish and navigation go on a narrow screen. Each design shows two screens. The palette buttons change all the phones at once.

Palette

IDrawer

A small top bar and a drawer holding the desktop rail and sidebar side by side. Edit and Publish stay on the banner, as on desktop.

Strengths
  • Closest to desktop, so there is one mental model and the least design and build work.
  • The drawer reuses the rail and sidebar components almost unchanged.
Tradeoffs
  • Publish sits at the top of the screen, the hardest place to reach with a thumb.
  • The banner toolbar gets crowded: crumbs, two icons, Done, and Publish.

JTab bar

A standard phone app layout. Bottom tabs for Pages, Search, Recent, Agents and Settings. The draft view adds a sticky bar with Done and Publish above the tabs.

Strengths
  • Publish is a large button in thumb reach and hard to miss.
  • Most familiar on phones. Settings and Agents are one tap away.
  • Spaces become chips, which scale better than a rail on a narrow screen.
Tradeoffs
  • The two bars at the bottom take about 130px from the page while editing.
  • Looks the least like the desktop layout.

KDock + sheet

The page fills the screen. A floating dock at the bottom holds the current folder and the page action. Tapping the folder slides up a sheet with spaces, navigation and the tree.

Strengths
  • The most room for reading and writing. The dock is the only chrome.
  • Navigation and Publish are both in thumb reach.
  • The sheet can be dragged partway up, then all the way.
Tradeoffs
  • Navigation is hidden until you open the sheet, so it's less discoverable.
  • The dock covers the end of the page unless content is padded for it.

LFloating button

Like I, with the main action in a floating button at bottom right: a pencil on the published view, Publish on the draft view.

Strengths
  • One obvious action per view, always in the same place.
  • The banner toolbar stays light: crumbs, History, Share, and Done.
Tradeoffs
  • A floating button can cover the end of a line or a table.
  • Navigation still comes from the drawer at the top, out of thumb reach.

Our starter is called Doughbert. It lives at the back of the fridge, left side, in the tall glass jar with the rubber band.

Feeding schedule

  1. Take it out the night before you bake.
  2. Discard all but 50 g.
  3. Feed 50 g flour (half whole wheat) and 50 g room-temperature water.
  4. Wait 4 to 8 hours, until it has doubled and smells tangy.
Grey liquid on top? That's hooch. Pour it off. It just means Doughbert is hungry.

Using the discard

Don't throw it out. It goes straight into Sunday pancakes or the flatbread in Weeknight dinners.

Our starter is called **Doughbert**. It lives at the back of the fridge, left side, in the tall glass jar with the rubber band.

## Feeding schedule

  1. Take it out the night before you bake.
  2. Discard all but 50 g.
  3. Feed 50 g flour (half whole wheat) and 50 g room-temperature water.
  4. Wait 4 to 8 hours, until it has doubled and smells tangy.
Grey liquid on top? That's hooch. Pour it off. It just means Doughbert is hungry.
